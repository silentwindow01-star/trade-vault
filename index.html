Overview

TradeEdge is a Monte Carlo trading simulator that models real statistical uncertainty in trading outcomes. Rather than showing a single "expected" result, it runs thousands of randomised simulations to show the full distribution of possible outcomes — from best case to worst case.

There are two independent tools:

⬡ Prop Firm Challenge

Models your probability of passing a funded account challenge given your strategy stats, challenge rules, and risk per trade. Outputs pass rate, expected cost to get funded, trades required, and a full comparison table across risk levels.

◈ Equity Curve Simulator

Projects your account equity over N trades using compounding or fixed position sizing. Shows best, worst, and median MC paths on an interactive chart, with probability breakdown of outcomes (loss / small / medium / large gain).

Tip: Both tools share the same Execution Accuracy parameter. Always set this honestly — using 100% accuracy with backtested stats will significantly overestimate real-world performance.

Workflow

1. Enter your strategy stats (Win Rate, Risk:Reward) — use your backtested or live tracked values.

2. Set Execution Accuracy honestly based on how consistently you execute your strategy in live conditions.

3. Configure challenge or account parameters, set your risk per trade, and click Run Simulation.

4. Review the results — pay particular attention to P90 values (90th percentile) which represent realistic worst-case planning scenarios.

Break-Even Analysis

The break-even bar shows the minimum return you need to achieve on your funded account to recover the total expected challenge cost.

Formula
Break-even Return = (Expected Cost to Fund) ÷ (Account Size × Profit Split)

E.g. if it cost you $1,000 in fees to get funded on a $100K account with 80% split: you need 1000 ÷ (100000 × 0.8) = 1.25% return just to break even.

Comparison Table

Shows all simulated risk levels side by side. Your chosen risk is highlighted. The table auto-generates 10 levels centered on your input with appropriate step sizes (0.1% steps below 1%, 0.25% steps from 1–3%, 0.5% steps above 3%). Click any row's risk level chip to switch the detail view without re-running.

Note: The 90% Confidence Cost column is fee × attemptsNeeded — the budget you should set aside if you want 90% confidence of getting funded. This is often significantly higher than Expected Cost.

The single MC path with the highest final balance. Represents the lucky tail — useful for understanding upside potential but not a realistic target.

Execution Accuracy

Execution Accuracy models the gap between your theoretical strategy statistics (from backtesting or paper trading) and your real-world live execution. In live trading, entries are late, stops get moved, winners get cut early, and emotional decisions degrade performance.

Set this to 100% only if you are running a fully automated strategy with no discretion. For discretionary traders, 70–85% is a realistic range.

Three-Layer Degradation Model

1. Miss Rate — A percentage of your setups are not executed cleanly. Instead of a proper trade, these become forced small losses (0.3–0.7R) due to late entries, FOMO reversals, or revenge trades.

Miss Rate Formula
miss_rate = (1 − accuracy)^1.5 × 0.4

2. Win Rate Drag — Poor execution pulls your effective win rate toward 50% (random noise). At perfect accuracy you trade your true WR; at poor accuracy you approach coin-flip territory.

Effective Win Rate
eff_WR = accuracy × true_WR + (1 − accuracy) × 0.50

3. RR Compression — Winners get cut short and losers get wider stops. Your effective RR degrades toward 0.5.

Effective Risk:Reward
eff_RR = accuracy × true_RR + (1 − accuracy) × 0.50

Additionally, even on winning trades, only a fraction of the potential RR is captured (scaled by accuracy), and losing trades can exceed 1R by up to 50% at very low accuracy.

Important: The three degradation mechanisms compound. A trader with 60% accuracy and 65% WR / 2R RR effectively trades closer to 57% WR and 1.3R RR — which dramatically changes the pass rate and equity projections.

Monte Carlo Simulation Engine

Monte Carlo (MC) simulation replaces the single "expected outcome" with thousands of randomised realities. Each simulation run independently draws win/loss outcomes for every trade using your input probabilities, producing a different equity path each time.

How Each Trade is Simulated

For each trade in each simulation path:

1. Apply execution accuracy degradation (miss rate check, WR drag, RR compression).

2. Draw a random number. If it falls below the effective win rate → win; otherwise → loss.

3. Calculate P&L: win → eff_RR × riskPct% or loss → −riskPct% (with potential RR variation based on accuracy).

4. Apply slippage and commissions (equity sim only).

5. Check drawdown rules (prop sim) or update equity (equity sim).

Prop Firm Sim — Two-Stage Architecture

The prop sim runs two separate MC loops to avoid a critical statistical error:

Stage 1 — Pass Rate

Each of N simulations runs exactly one challenge attempt. passes ÷ N gives the true per-attempt pass probability. No retries.

Stage 2 — Trades to Fund

A separate sim retries until funded (up to 100 attempts) to count total cumulative trades. This only runs when attempts needed > 1.

Why separate? If retries were included in pass rate calculation, even a 5% per-attempt pass rate would show near-100% "pass rate" after 100 retries. The two-stage design ensures pass rate = single-attempt probability, while total trades accounts for real cumulative effort.

P90 vs Mean

Many results are reported at P90 (90th percentile) rather than the mean. This means 90% of simulation outcomes were at or below this value. P90 is a more conservative and actionable planning figure — if you budget for P90, you will be covered in 9 out of 10 scenarios.

The mean (average) is better for understanding the central tendency, but it is skewed by extreme outlier runs. For financial planning — especially challenge fee budgets — always use P90.

Drawdown Types

The drawdown type controls how the max drawdown limit is measured. Different prop firms use different methods — choosing the right type in the simulator is critical for accurate pass rate estimates.

Initial (Static)

Drawdown measured from the starting balance (fixed reference point). If your account starts at $100K with 10% max DD, you breach at $90K — regardless of whether you hit $110K first. The limit never moves up. Used by: FTMO, The5%ers.

Trailing (Intraday)

The drawdown floor follows your equity peak on every trade. If you reach $105K on a $100K account with 10% trailing DD, your new floor is $94,500. Any further profit raises the floor further. Strictest rule — a big early win can make later trading harder. Used by: Apex, TopStep.

EOD Trailing

Trailing, but the high-water mark only locks in at the end of each trading day (every 5 trades in the sim). Intraday equity swings don't move the floor — only closing balance matters. More forgiving than intraday trailing; gives room for intraday drawdown recovery before the floor updates.

Daily Drawdown (All Types)

An additional intraday limit that applies on top of any max DD type. Measured from the balance at the start of each day. A 5% daily DD means you cannot lose more than 5% of account in a single day — even if your total DD limit has not been reached.

Impact on Pass Rate

For the same strategy and risk level, expect roughly: Initial > EOD Trailing > Intraday Trailing in terms of pass rate. Trailing drawdowns are harder because a string of early winners raises the floor, leaving less room to absorb future losses.

Tip: If you are unsure which type your prop firm uses, check their FAQ for keywords like "trailing maximum drawdown", "static drawdown", or "end-of-day trailing". Using the wrong type in the sim can produce significantly misleading pass rate estimates.

TermDefinition
